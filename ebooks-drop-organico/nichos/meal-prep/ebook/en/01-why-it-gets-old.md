# Part 1 · The System

# 1 · Why Meal Prep Gets Old by Wednesday

> "…the same ingredients precooked and rearranged in slightly different ways got really old really fast."
> — reader review

Most people don't quit meal prep because of the cooking. They quit on Wednesday, standing at the fridge, looking at box three of the same chicken and rice.

Here's what usually happens. You cook one protein with one seasoning. You make one carb. You steam one vegetable. You split it all into five boxes. Monday is fine. Tuesday is fine. By Wednesday your mouth knows exactly what's coming, and you order a burrito instead. Thursday's box goes in the trash on Saturday.

Moving the same food around the plate doesn't fix it. If the chicken tastes like lemon and garlic on Monday, it tastes like lemon and garlic on Thursday, whether it sits on rice or in a wrap.

What changes a meal the most is the flavor you add at the end: the sauce, the acid, the spice. So this book cooks the big parts plain-ish and puts the flavor in the sauces.

## The plate formula

Every lunch and dinner in this book is built the same way:

**1 protein + 1 base + 1 vegetable + 1 sauce**

| Slot | What it does | Examples in this book | How it's cooked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | Fills you up and carries most of the protein | Lemon-garlic chicken thighs, turkey taco crumble, baked salmon, crispy tofu | Seasoned lightly so it fits several sauces |
| Base | Energy and bulk | Oven rice, quinoa, roasted sweet potato, pasta, tortillas | Cooked to reheat well (Chapter 13) |
| Vegetable | Color, crunch, fiber | Charred broccoli, fajita peppers, slaw, cucumber-tomato salad | Roasted, or raw and added on the day |
| Sauce | Decides what the plate tastes like | Chipotle-lime yogurt, lighter teriyaki, lemon-tahini | Made cold in 5 minutes, stored apart |

The protein, base and vegetable are your batch. The sauce is your steering wheel.

## The four flavor families

The 12 sauces are grouped into four flavor families, three sauces each. Each family has proteins, bases and vegetables that fit it.

| Family | Code | Tastes like | Sauces | Fits well with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mexican-inspired | **MEX** | Lime, chipotle, cumin, cilantro | Chipotle-Lime Yogurt · Tomatillo Salsa Verde · Quick Red Enchilada Sauce | Rice, tortillas, sweet potato, fajita peppers |
| Asian-inspired | **ASI** | Soy, ginger, sesame, chili | Lighter Teriyaki · Peanut-Ginger · Gochujang-Honey | Rice, quinoa, pasta, broccoli, slaw, green beans |
| Mediterranean | **MED** | Lemon, garlic, herbs, yogurt | Tzatziki · Lemon-Tahini · Chimichurri | Quinoa, rice, potatoes, cucumber-tomato salad |
| American comfort | **COM** | Smoky, sweet-tangy, creamy | Smoky BBQ · Honey-Mustard Yogurt · Creamy Tomato-Garlic | Potatoes, sweet potato, pasta, green beans |

Some proteins fit every family. The lemon-garlic chicken thighs, the baked chicken breast and the salmon are seasoned so any sauce works on them. Others fit two or three families. The turkey taco crumble is MEX only. Each recipe card tells you which.

## One tray, three different plates

This is how Week 1 uses one tray of lemon-garlic chicken thighs with three sauces:

| Day | Protein | Base | Vegetable | Sauce | Family |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plate 1 | Lemon-garlic chicken | Oven rice | Fajita peppers | Chipotle-lime yogurt | MEX |
| Plate 2 | Lemon-garlic chicken | Oven rice | Charred broccoli | Lighter teriyaki | ASI |
| Plate 3 | Lemon-garlic chicken | Roasted sweet potato | Charred broccoli | Lemon-tahini | MED |

Same chicken. Same Sunday. Three meals that don't taste alike. Add a second protein (the turkey crumble) and Week 1 gets to five different plates from two proteins, two bases, two vegetables and three sauces.

That's the whole trick. You cook a small number of parts and change the combination.

## Two rules that keep the week interesting

**Rule 1: no sauce two days in a row.** If Monday is chipotle-lime, Tuesday is something else. This one rule does most of the work.

**Rule 2: something crunchy at least twice a week.** Roasted food softens in the fridge. A raw slaw or a cucumber-tomato salad added on the day gives the box a different texture. Chapter 4 shows how to store those parts apart.

## What "48 plates" and "Mix & Match" mean

Chapter 16 has 48 signature plates: combinations worked out in full, with calories, protein, allergens and cost for the assembled plate. Twelve per flavor family.

It also has a Mix & Match chart. It lists 2,106 pairings that follow the flavor-family rules above. Those pairings were not each cooked one by one. They're a map of what should go together, built from the rules. You'll use it once you're ready to plan your own weeks (Week 4 walks you through it).

## Your action: the 5-box test

Plan any five boxes, on paper, before you cook anything. Use the parts from the tables above or the ones you already like to cook.

| Box | Protein | Base | Vegetable | Sauce | Family |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | | | | | |
| Tue | | | | | |
| Wed | | | | | |
| Thu | | | | | |
| Fri | | | | | |

Now check it:

- [ ] No sauce appears two days in a row.
- [ ] At least two boxes have something raw or crunchy.
- [ ] You use no more than 2 proteins, 2 bases and 2 vegetables in total. (More than that and Sunday gets long.)
- [ ] At least two different flavor families.

If a box fails a check, change the sauce first. It's the cheapest fix and it changes the most.
