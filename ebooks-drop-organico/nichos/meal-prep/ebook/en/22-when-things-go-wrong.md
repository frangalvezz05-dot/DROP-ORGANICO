# 22 · When Things Go Wrong

> "If only someone had properly proofread this work and written better instructions."
> — reader review

<!-- Frase F21 (frases.md), fragmento literal. Reseña en Goodreads de "Batch Cooking" (Keda Black): https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/44287434-batch-cooking -->

Something will go wrong on one of your Sundays. The rice will come out crunchy or the tofu will be soft. That's not a reason to stop. Most fixes take 5 minutes, and the next batch comes out better because you know why.

Each problem below has three parts: **why it happens**, **fix it now**, **next time**.

## Proteins

**1 · Chicken is dry or rubbery** (P1, P3)
- *Why:* it went past 165 °F / 74 °C, or the pieces were uneven so the thin ones overcooked.
- *Fix it now:* slice it and toss with a spoon of pan juices or 1 Tbsp water before it goes in the box. Reheat at 70% power, covered.
- *Next time:* pound breasts to an even thickness. Pull it when the thermometer says 165 °F, not when the timer ends.

**2 · Salmon has white goo on top, or smells strong by day 3** (P8)
- *Why:* the white is albumin, pushed out by hot, fast cooking. The smell is age: cooked fish fades fastest.
- *Fix it now:* scrape the white off; it's safe. Past day 3 or if it smells off: throw it out.
- *Next time:* bake at 375 °F and check a few minutes earlier. Eat salmon on days 1-2 only and freeze any extra the same day.

**3 · Meatballs fall apart, or come out dry** (P6)
- *Why:* falling apart = the mix was too wet. Dry = overmixed or overbaked.
- *Fix it now:* warm dry ones in a covered pan with a spoon of sauce.
- *Next time:* 1 more Tbsp breadcrumbs and 10 minutes in the fridge before rolling. Mix with your hands only until it holds.

**4 · Pulled pork won't shred** (P7)
- *Why:* it's safe at 145 °F but only shreds at 195-205 °F, when the tough tissue melts.
- *Fix it now:* re-cover and give it 30-45 more minutes. Check again.
- *Next time:* start it earlier. Week 4 puts it in 4½ hours before the session.

**5 · Tofu is soft, not crispy** (P9)
- *Why:* not pressed enough, too crowded, or reheated in the microwave.
- *Fix it now:* 6-8 minutes at 400 °F / 205 °C in the oven or air fryer.
- *Next time:* press the full 20 minutes, pat it dry, use 2 pans so cubes don't touch.

**6 · Lentils are still hard after 30 minutes** (P5)
- *Why:* old lentils take longer.
- *Fix it now:* add ½ cup water and simmer 10 more minutes.
- *Next time:* buy a fresh bag and check the date. Too wet instead? Simmer uncovered 5 minutes.

## Bases & vegetables

**7 · Rice is crunchy, or gummy** (B1)
- *Why:* crunchy = steam leaked through the foil. Gummy = too much water, or it wasn't rinsed.
- *Fix it now:* crunchy: 2 Tbsp hot water, re-cover tight, 10 more minutes. Gummy: spread on a pan, 5 minutes in the oven to dry.
- *Next time:* crimp the foil hard all round. Rinse 30 seconds.

**8 · Pasta is mushy by Wednesday** (B5)
- *Why:* it was fully cooked on Sunday, then cooked again in the microwave.
- *Fix it now:* reheat it with the sauce, briefly, and eat it. It's safe, just soft.
- *Next time:* drain it 1-2 minutes before the package time and toss with oil.

**9 · Roasted vegetables or sweet potatoes are soggy and pale** (V1-V4, B3, B4)
- *Why:* the pan was crowded, the pieces were wet, or the oven was too cool. Crowded food steams.
- *Fix it now:* drain any liquid. 5 minutes in a hot oven or air fryer before eating.
- *Next time:* dry the vegetables, one layer, pieces not touching. Use a second pan. It's the most common fix in this book.

**10 · Potatoes went soft in the microwave** (B3)
- *Why:* the microwave steams them.
- *Fix it now:* air fryer or oven, 400 °F / 205 °C, 8-10 minutes.
- *Next time:* if you only have a microwave at work, pick sweet potatoes (B4), rice or quinoa for that box.

## Sauces & breakfasts

**11 · A sauce split, went grainy, or set solid** (S1, S5, S7, S8, S12)
- *Why:* yogurt sauces split when heated. Tahini and peanut butter tighten when cold. A cream sauce splits over high heat.
- *Fix it now:* yogurt sauces: never heat them; stir in 1 Tbsp yogurt. Tahini or peanut: whisk in 1 tsp water at a time. Creamy tomato: whisk in 1 Tbsp warm water off the heat.
- *Next time:* keep sauces in their own cups, cold, and add them after reheating.

**12 · Egg bites are wet in the middle, or rubbery** (D1, D2)
- *Why:* wet = underbaked or too much spinach water. Rubbery = overbaked.
- *Fix it now:* wet: back in the oven 3-5 minutes until a knife comes out clean and the center reads 160 °F / 71 °C. Don't eat them underdone.
- *Next time:* squeeze the spinach hard. Start checking at 20 minutes.

## The week itself

**13 · Thursday's box tastes flat**
- *Why:* flavors fade in the fridge. It's not spoiled, it's tired.
- *Fix it now:* a squeeze of lime or lemon, a pinch of salt, fresh herbs or scallion, right before eating. The sauce goes on cold or warmed, never stored on the food.
- *Next time:* store sauce, slaw and salad apart (Chapter 4).

**14 · You're running late on the Sunday Sheet**
- *Why:* the first run is always slower. Or the oven runs cool.
- *Fix it now:* never skip a done signal to catch up. Skip a cold sauce instead (make it Monday night; it takes 5 minutes) or roll the burritos later.
- *Next time:* read the sheet the night before. Put an oven thermometer in the oven: if the dial says 425 °F and the oven is at 390 °F, every tray runs long.

**15 · Friday's box is still frozen, or you forgot to move it**
- *Why:* it needed the full day in the fridge.
- *Fix it now:* thaw it in the microwave on defrost, then reheat to 165 °F / 74 °C and eat it right away (FSIS). Never thaw it on the counter.
- *Next time:* set a phone reminder for Thursday morning. The printable fridge card has one too.

## The rule above all of them

If a box smells off, looks slimy, or you're not sure how old it is: **when in doubt, throw it out.** One lost lunch costs less than a sick week. (USDA FSIS)
