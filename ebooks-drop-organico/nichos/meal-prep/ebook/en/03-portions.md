# 3 · Portions That Add Up

> "recipe portions are inconsistent. Sometimes we got a scant 4 servings, sometimes 6, sometimes more."

"Serves 4" is a guess. It depends on whose plate, how hungry, and how big the potatoes were. When you cook for a week, a wrong guess shows up on Thursday as one box with half the chicken.

This book doesn't use "serves". It uses grams and boxes.

## What every recipe tells you

Each recipe card starts with one line like this:

**Makes:** 5 portions · about 700 g cooked in total · about 140 g per portion

That line answers two questions before you start: how much food comes out, and how many boxes it fills.

The calories and protein on the card are for one of those portions. That means one fifth of the batch if it makes 5, one tenth if it makes 10.

## The batch-divide method

Cooked weight is never exact. Chicken loses water in the oven. Rice soaks it up. Ten minutes more or less changes the number.

So don't trust the raw weight to tell you the box weight. Weigh what came out.

1. Put a bowl on your kitchen scale and press **tare** so it reads 0.
2. Tip the whole cooked batch into the bowl. Write down the weight.
3. Divide by the number of boxes. That's your grams per box.
4. Put an empty box on the scale, tare, and fill it to that number. Repeat.

Example: the chicken comes out at 690 g and you need 5 boxes. 690 ÷ 5 = 138 g per box.

Every box ends up with the same share of the batch. And the calories and protein per portion on the card stay right, because each box really is one fifth.

Weighing 10 boxes takes a few minutes once the scale is out. It's the step most people skip and the one that stops the half-empty Thursday box.

## The reference portion

When you build your own plates (Path B), start from this box. It's the size used across the 48 signature plates. You can scale it up or down, but change one slot at a time.

| Slot | Per box (cooked) | Roughly from (raw or dry) | Kitchen picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | 130-140 g (about 4½-5 oz) | 180 g raw boneless meat | A deck of cards, a bit thicker |
| Base | 150-170 g cooked rice, quinoa or pasta | 60 g dry rice or quinoa, 70 g dry pasta | About 1 cup cooked |
| Base, potato | 170-200 g roasted | about 200 g raw | A fist and a half |
| Vegetable | 100 g roasted, or 80-100 g raw | 120-140 g raw before roasting | A packed cup |
| Sauce | 40-50 g | — | About 3 tablespoons |

The raw column is a rough guide for shopping. The cooked column is what you weigh. If they disagree, the scale wins.

## ×1 or ×2: how many boxes

Every shopping list and every Sunday Sheet has two columns.

| Column | Boxes | Covers | Breakfasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **×1** | 10 | One person: lunch + dinner, Monday to Friday. Or two people: lunch only. | 5 |
| **×2** | 20 | Two people: lunch + dinner, Monday to Friday. | 10 |

The amounts in the ×2 column are worked out for you. Two things don't double:

- **Cooking time.** A pan with twice the food doesn't need twice the minutes. It needs the same done signal. Use more pans, not more time.
- **Pan space.** Food has to sit in one layer to roast. If it doesn't fit, use a third pan or cook a second round. The ×2 sheets note where that happens.

## How the boxes split across the week

With the ×1 column, 10 boxes come out on Sunday. They don't all go in the fridge.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boxes | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Stored in | fridge | fridge | fridge | fridge | **freezer** |

The two Friday boxes go to the freezer on Sunday. Chapter 4 explains why, and when to move them back.
