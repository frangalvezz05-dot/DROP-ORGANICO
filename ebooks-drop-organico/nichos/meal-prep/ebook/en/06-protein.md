High-protein food has a reputation: dry chicken, plain rice, no sauce, eaten because you have to. That's not the food in this book. These are normal plates, the kind you'd order, with the numbers worked out so you don't have to guess.

This chapter shows where those numbers come from, how to read them, and how to add them up when you build your own plate.

## Where the numbers come from

Every calorie and protein figure in this book is **calculated**, not estimated.

1. **The data.** USDA FoodData Central, the SR Legacy dataset. It's the US Department of Agriculture's public food composition database.
2. **The weights.** Each ingredient is entered in grams, **raw or dry, as you buy it**: raw chicken, dry rice, raw broccoli. It's matched to the USDA entry in the same state ("chicken thigh, meat only, raw", not "roasted").
3. **Everything counts.** Oil, sauce, honey, cheese. If it goes in the pan, it's in the total.
4. **Divide.** The batch total is divided by the number of portions on the card.
5. **Round.** Calories to the nearest 10. Protein to the nearest gram.

**Why raw weights?** Raw is what you buy and can weigh before cooking. Cooked weight changes with oven time, because cooking mostly drives off water. The protein stays in the pan. So a total built from raw weights stays the same however long the chicken roasted. That's also why Chapter 3 tells you to divide the cooked batch evenly: each box then carries its share of the raw total.

**What this can't do.**

- **Some fat melts off.** With fattier cuts (thighs, pork shoulder), part of the fat stays in the pan. The real calories in your box can be a little lower than the card.
- **Your brand isn't the USDA average.** For packaged foods (yogurt, tortillas, sauces, protein-added products), the label on your package is more accurate than our figure. If they differ a lot, use the label.

## How to read the line on a card

Every recipe and plate shows a line like this:

**Per portion:** 560 kcal · 48 g protein · 56 g carbs · 15 g fat · *USDA FoodData Central*

- **Per portion** means one box, one fifth of a 5-portion batch, split as in Chapter 3.
- **kcal** is food energy, what US labels call Calories.
- **Protein, carbs, fat** are in grams.

These numbers describe the food. They don't say how much of it you should eat. That depends on you, and it's a conversation for a registered dietitian or your doctor (see Start Here).

## One plate, worked out

Here's a simplified plate, calculated the same way as every recipe. It isn't a recipe card. The sauce is only its yogurt base, so the steps are easy to follow.

| Part | Raw or dry, per portion | kcal | Protein |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh, boneless, skinless | 180 g raw | 220 | 35 g |
| White rice, long-grain | 60 g dry | 220 | 4 g |
| Broccoli + olive oil | 100 g raw + 7 g oil | 100 | 3 g |
| Plain nonfat Greek yogurt (sauce base) | 50 g | 30 | 5 g |
| **Plate, calculated as a whole** | | **560** | **48 g** |

*Calculated with USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy. Carbs 56 g, fat 15 g, fiber 3 g.*

The rows add up to 570 kcal and 47 g, not 560 and 48. That's rounding: each row is rounded on its own. The bottom line is calculated from the unrounded numbers, so it's the one to trust.

## Swap the protein, see the real number

Now replace the chicken with 150 g of raw firm tofu. Same rice, broccoli, oil and yogurt.

| Plate | kcal | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| With chicken thigh (180 g raw) | 560 | 48 g |
| With firm tofu (150 g raw) | 560 | 38 g |

Plant-based plates in this book usually land lower in protein than meat plates of the same size. We print the real figure for each one. We don't average it away or round it up.

If you want more protein on a plant plate, the usual moves are a bigger protein portion, a yogurt- or tofu-based sauce, or edamame on the side. Each changes the calories too, so recalculate with the sheet below.

## Allergens on a combined plate

Each component card lists its allergens, from the 9 major food allergens recognized in the US: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame.

When you combine parts, **the plate has every allergen of every part.** A plain chicken thigh has none. Add rice: still none. Add teriyaki made with regular soy sauce: now it has **soy and wheat**.

Common ones to watch in this book:

| Ingredient | Usually contains | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Soy sauce | Soy, wheat | Tamari is often wheat-free. Check the label. |
| Gochujang | Soy, often wheat | Varies by brand |
| Tahini, sesame oil | Sesame | |
| Peanut-ginger sauce | Peanuts, soy | |
| Tzatziki, yogurt sauces, feta | Milk | |
| Tortillas, pasta | Wheat | |
| Egg bites | Eggs, milk | |
