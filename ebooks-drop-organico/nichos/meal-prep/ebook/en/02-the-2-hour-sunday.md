Sunday is your day off. If meal prep eats all of it, you'll stop doing it.

Most lost Sundays look alike. You read recipe one and start it. Then you read recipe two and find out it needed the oven at a different temperature. The oven sits empty while you chop. Three things finish at the same minute, or one thing finishes 40 minutes after everything else. You don't know how much goes in each box, so the last box is half empty.

None of that is about cooking skill. It's about order.

The Sunday Sheet fixes the order. It takes every recipe for the week and merges them into one timeline on one page. You read the sheet, not the recipes.

## What's on the sheet, top to bottom

**1. The header.** Week number. Cooking for 1 or 2. Number of boxes. Oven temperature. Estimated time, which is about 2 hours for one person's week. Plus an empty box where you write your real time.

**2. Before minute 0.** About 10 minutes of setup. Clear the counter. Empty the dishwasher or the drying rack. Line the sheet pans. Take out the scale, the thermometer and every container you'll fill. Set the containers on the table with their lids off.

**3. The timeline.** Four blocks, always in the same order:

| Order | Block | Why it goes here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **OVEN** | It takes longest and needs you least. Start it first and it works while you do the rest. |
| 2 | **STOVE** | It needs you at the pan, in short bursts. It fits in while the oven runs. |
| 3 | **COLD** | Sauces, slaw, cutting raw vegetables. No heat. Fills the gaps while hot food cooks and cools. |
| 4 | **ASSEMBLY** | Weighing into boxes, labeling, into the fridge or freezer. Last, when everything is done. |

The blocks overlap. The oven block starts first, but the stove and cold work happen while it's still running. The sheet marks those tasks with **⏱ while the oven runs**.

**4. The assembly table.** How many grams of each part go in each box, for 1 or 2 people. Which boxes go to the fridge. Which go to the freezer (the day-5 rule, Chapter 4).

## How to read one row

Each line of the timeline has five columns:

| Clock | Station | Do this | Done when | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | OVEN | Heat oven to 425 °F / 220 °C. Line 2 sheet pans with parchment. | Oven signals it's hot | — |
| 0:05 | PREP ⏱ | Cut sweet potatoes into ¾-inch (2 cm) cubes. | Cubes about the same size | see list |
| 0:15 | OVEN | Chicken and sweet potatoes in, on separate pans. Set a timer for each pan. | — | — |
| 0:20 | STOVE ⏱ | Brown the ground turkey in a large skillet, breaking it up. | No pink left. 165 °F / 74 °C | see list |
| 0:35 | COLD ⏱ | Stir the chipotle-lime yogurt sauce. Spoon into small cups. | Smooth, no lumps | 50 g per cup |
| … | ASSEMBLY | Weigh each part into the boxes. Sauce stays in its cup. | Every box closed and labeled | see assembly table |

*These rows show the layout. The real minutes and amounts for each week are in Chapters 8 to 11.*

**Clock** is minutes from when you start, not time of day. If you start at 10:00, 0:35 means 10:35.

**Done when** is the most important column. Ovens differ, pans differ, cuts of meat differ. A timer tells you when to look. The done signal tells you when to stop. For meat, the signal is a temperature, read with a thermometer in the thickest part.

## Safe internal temperatures

These are the USDA FSIS minimums. They're printed on every sheet where meat or fish cooks.

| Food | Safe minimum |
|---|---|
| Chicken and turkey: whole pieces **and ground** | 165 °F / 74 °C |
| Ground beef and ground pork | 160 °F / 71 °C |
| Pork chops, roasts, shoulder | 145 °F / 63 °C, then rest 3 minutes |
| Fish, including salmon | 145 °F / 63 °C, or flesh flakes easily and is opaque |
| Egg dishes (egg bites, casseroles) | 160 °F / 71 °C |

Ground turkey trips people up. It's poultry, so it's 165 °F, not 160 °F.

Pulled pork is different again. It's safe at 145 °F, but it only pulls apart when you cook it much longer, so its recipe gives a higher target for texture.

## Five habits that save time

1. **Read the whole sheet the night before.** It takes 5 minutes. You'll spot what needs thawing or what you forgot to buy.
2. **One timer per pan, with a name.** Phone timers can be labeled: "chicken", "rice". Three unlabeled beeps in a row is how food burns.
3. **Tick each row as you finish it.** Use a pencil. If you get interrupted, you know where you were.
4. **Wash as you wait.** Each ⏱ gap longer than 5 minutes with nothing listed is a good time for the cutting board and bowls.
5. **Don't start a new block early because you're bored.** The order is there so the oven and the fridge aren't fighting each other.

## About "about 2 hours"

The plan weeks are built to fit a cooking session of about 2 hours for one person. That's a target, not a stopwatch result you're guaranteed to hit. Your first Sunday will likely take longer. Cooking for two adds mostly cutting and portioning time. The oven time stays close to the same, as long as the food still fits in a single layer on the pans. If pieces overlap, they steam instead of browning. Use another pan.
