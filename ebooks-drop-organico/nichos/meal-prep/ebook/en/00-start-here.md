That line comes from someone who bought a different meal prep book. It names the problem this one is built for. You don't need more recipes. You need to know what to cook on Sunday, in what order, how much of it, and how long it keeps.

So this is a system first and a recipe book second.

## What's inside

**Part 1 · The System** (Chapters 1-7). How a plate is built, how to read your Sunday Sheet, portions, storage, cost, protein, pantry. About 20 pages. You read it once.

**Part 2 · The 4-Week Plan** (Chapters 8-11). Each week is one Sunday Sheet, one shopping list sorted by store aisle (for 1 or 2 people) and five plates.

**Part 3 · Recipes** (Chapters 12-19). 60 recipes, written as parts you combine: 10 proteins, 6 bases, 6 vegetables, 12 sauces, plus 8 breakfasts, 10 one-pot freezer meals and 8 snacks. Then 48 signature plates and the Mix & Match chart.

**Part 4 · Reference** (Chapters 20-23). Storage chart, macro-cost-allergen index, fixes for things that go wrong, blank templates.

Every recipe card shows the same facts in the same spot: yield in grams and boxes, calories and protein per portion, allergens, days in the fridge and freezer, how to reheat, and what it goes with.

## Pick your path

**Path A · Follow Week 1**
For you if you want zero decisions this time.
Read Chapter 2 (the sheet) and Chapter 4 (storage). That's about 15 minutes. Then go to Chapter 8, shop from the list and cook from the sheet.
You end Sunday with 10 boxes and 5 breakfasts. That's lunch and dinner Monday to Friday for one person, or lunch for two.

**Path B · Build your own plates**
For you if you've prepped before and know what you like.
Read Chapters 1, 3 and 6. Then go to the sauces (Chapter 15) and the Mix & Match chart (Chapter 16). Plan your week on the blank sheet in Chapter 23.

**Path C · Freezer only**
For you if you can't give up a Sunday right now, or you get tired of the same box by day three.
Read Chapter 4. Then go to the Freezer Block (Chapter 18): 10 one-pot meals you cook on any free evening, portion and freeze. You build a stock and take out one box at a time.

Not sure? Take Path A. Week 1 uses the parts the rest of the book builds on.

## What "about 2 hours" means

The plan weeks are designed around a Sunday cooking session of about 2 hours for one person's week. That's a design target. It is not a promise.

Your first Sunday will probably run longer. You're reading as you go. Your oven may run cooler than the dial says. Shopping isn't counted, and neither is the last round of dishes.

Time your first Sunday and write the real number on your sheet. Your number is the one that matters. Write it down each week and see how it moves once the steps stop being new.

## Before you cook: three notes

**1. This is not medical or nutrition advice.** Each recipe shows calories and protein per portion, calculated from USDA data (Chapter 6). The book does not tell you how much to eat. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or follow a diet set by a professional, talk to a registered dietitian or your doctor before you change how you eat.

**2. Allergens.** Each recipe lists which of the 9 major food allergens recognized in the US it contains: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. Brands differ. Soy sauce usually contains wheat. Many tortillas and some gochujang do too. Products can also be made on shared lines. Read the label of the package you actually buy. This book can't see it.

**3. Food safety.** Storage times and cooking temperatures follow USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) guidance. Chapter 4 has the rules and the sources. Use a thermometer. If something smells off or you're unsure how old it is, follow the FSIS rule: when in doubt, throw it out.
