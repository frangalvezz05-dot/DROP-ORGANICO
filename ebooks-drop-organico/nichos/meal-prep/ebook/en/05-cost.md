Meat is the biggest line on almost any meal prep receipt. A plan that quietly asks for 4 or 5 pounds of it every week gets expensive fast, and you only notice at the register.

This chapter gives you a way to know the cost before you shop. It also tells you how much meat each week of the plan uses, so nothing is hidden.

## Prices change. Read our numbers that way.

Food prices move every week. They differ by store, by city and by brand. Any price printed in a book is old by the time you read it.

So every cost in this book comes with a **store type, a region and a date**. Treat it as a reference point, not a quote. Your receipt is the real number. The method below lets you work out your own in a few minutes.

## The method: cost per portion

**Cost per portion = price you paid for the package ÷ number of portions you get from it**

That's it for anything you use up in one week: meat, a bag of broccoli, a tub of yogurt.

For things you use a little of at a time (oil, spices, soy sauce), work out the share:

**Cost of what you used = package price ÷ package size × amount used**

Example with made-up numbers, only to show the math:

| Item | Package price | Gives | Cost per portion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pack of chicken thighs | $10.00 | 5 portions | $10.00 ÷ 5 = **$2.00** |
| Bag of rice, 2 lb (907 g) | $3.00 | about 15 portions of 60 g | $3.00 ÷ 15 = **$0.20** |
| Olive oil: 7 g from a 500 g bottle | $8.00 | — | $8.00 ÷ 500 × 7 = **$0.11** |

*These are not real prices. They're round numbers so the steps are easy to follow.*

Add up the parts of one plate and you have the plate's cost. Add up the shopping list, plus the share of pantry items used, and you have the week.

## Read the shelf tag, not the sticker

US grocery shelf tags usually show a **unit price** next to the price: per pound, per ounce, per count. It's smaller print, often on the left. That's the number to compare.

The big pack isn't always cheaper per pound. The store brand often is. Compare unit prices, not package prices.

## Five ways the plan keeps cost down

1. **Stretch ground meat with lentils.** The Beef & Lentil Power Mince replaces part of the beef with cooked lentils. You get more portions per pound of beef, plus fiber, and the sauce carries the flavor.
2. **Thighs before breasts.** Boneless thighs are often cheaper per pound and stay juicier when reheated. Check the unit price at your store.
3. **Use whole packages.** The recipes aim to use standard package sizes, and each shopping list notes leftovers you can use the next week. A half-used pack of meat at the back of the fridge is money in the trash.
4. **Frozen vegetables are fine.** Frozen broccoli or green beans cost the same in January as in June and never wilt in the drawer. They roast well if you spread them out and give them a few extra minutes.
5. **Buy the pantry once.** Spices and sauces are a big cost the first week and almost nothing after. Chapter 7 lists what to keep, so the weekly list stays short.

What the plan skips: precut vegetables. They save time, and they often cost noticeably more per pound. Cutting is built into the Sunday Sheet, during oven time.
