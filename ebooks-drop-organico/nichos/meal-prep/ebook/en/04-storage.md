# 4 · Will It Last Until Thursday? Storage, Freezing, Reheating

Two things send prepped food to the trash. You're not sure it's still safe. Or it's safe but tastes tired. This chapter handles both.

One of the most upvoted questions of the year in a large meal prep forum was a thread titled: "is it fine to store my food like this for four-five days?" Four is fine. Five is where the answer changes.

## The day-5 rule

USDA FSIS says cooked leftovers keep **3 to 4 days** in the fridge, at 40 °F (4 °C) or colder.

Count from Sunday:

| Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cook (day 0) | day 1 | day 2 | day 3 | day 4 | **day 5** |

Thursday is day 4. Friday is day 5, outside the window.

So the rule in this book is:

**Anything you'll eat on day 5 goes in the freezer on Sunday.**

Then on **Thursday morning**, move Friday's boxes from the freezer to the fridge. They thaw during the day and they're ready for Friday. Every Sunday Sheet ends with this step, and the printable fridge card has a Thursday reminder.

Two more rules:

- **Salmon is eaten on day 1 or 2.** It's safe for the same 3-4 days, but cooked fish dries out and smells stronger as days pass. The plan puts it at the start of the week.
- **Freeze on Sunday, not on Wednesday.** Freezing doesn't reset the clock. A box that has already spent three days in the fridge comes out of the freezer as a day-3 box.

## Cool it fast

Bacteria grow fastest between 40 °F and 140 °F (4-60 °C). FSIS calls it the Danger Zone. The rule is to get cooked food into the fridge **within 2 hours** of cooking. If the room is above 90 °F (32 °C), within 1 hour.

You don't have to wait for food to cool before it goes in the fridge. FSIS says hot food can go straight in. What matters is that it cools quickly, and that means shallow.

- **Spread it out.** A deep pot of chili cools slowly in the middle. Split it into shallow containers, about 2 inches (5 cm) deep or less.
- **Leave space.** Don't stack ten hot boxes in a tight block. Leave gaps so cold air moves.
- **Lids on loosely for the first 20-30 minutes**, then close them.

### Rice needs extra care

Rice can carry spores of a bacterium called *Bacillus cereus*. Cooking doesn't kill the spores. If cooked rice sits warm on the counter for hours, they can grow and make a toxin that reheating won't destroy.

So, as soon as the rice is done:

1. Spread it in a thin layer on a sheet pan or in wide, shallow containers.
2. Let the steam escape for about 10 minutes.
3. Into the fridge. **All of it within 2 hours of coming off the heat.**

The same goes for quinoa and pasta. Cooling rice fast also keeps the grains apart, so it reheats better.

## Freezer basics

- Set the freezer at **0 °F (-18 °C)**. At that temperature, food stays safe for as long as it stays frozen.
- **Quality** is a different clock. Most cooked dishes taste best if eaten within **2-3 months** (soups, stews, casseroles) and cooked meat within about **2-6 months**. Each recipe card gives its own number. Write the date on every box.
- **Thaw in the fridge.** A single box needs about a day. Never thaw on the counter.
- If you forget: thaw in the microwave on defrost, then reheat and eat right away.

## Reheat to 165 °F

FSIS says leftovers should be reheated to **165 °F (74 °C)**. Microwaves heat unevenly, so:

- Cover the box loosely (a lid set on top, not snapped shut).
- Heat, stir or turn the food, heat again.
- Check the thickest piece. Steaming all the way through is the visual sign. A thermometer is the sure one.

## Keep the taste: store parts apart

Food tastes tired by Thursday for reasons you can control.

| Part | What goes wrong | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sauces and dressings | They soak into rice and pasta, which turn soggy | Separate small cup. Add after reheating. |
| Yogurt sauces (tzatziki, chipotle-lime) | They split and turn grainy if heated | Never heat. Add cold, on top of the hot food. |
| Raw crunch (slaw, cucumber-tomato salad) | Goes limp, gets watery | Slaw: own container, keeps its crunch a few days. Cucumber-tomato: cut on the day, 3 minutes. |
| Pasta | Turns mushy | Cook 2 minutes short of the package time, toss with a little oil. |
| Roasted potatoes | Go soft in the microwave | Reheat in an air fryer or oven, 5-8 minutes. |
| Rice | Dries out and goes hard | Sprinkle 1 tablespoon of water on top, cover, then heat. |
| Chicken | Dries out | 1 tablespoon of water or sauce, cover, medium power. |

**The two-zone box.** Many boxes have a hot side and a cold side. Take the sauce cup and any raw parts out. Heat the rest. Put the cold parts back on top. It takes 20 seconds and it's the difference between a Thursday lunch and a sad one.

**Wake it up.** Flavors fade in the fridge. A squeeze of lime or lemon, a pinch of salt or a few fresh herbs right before you eat bring them back. The recipe cards suggest one for each plate.
